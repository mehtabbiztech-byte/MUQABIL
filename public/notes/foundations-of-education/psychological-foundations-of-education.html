Psychological Foundations of Education

Understanding how humans learn, develop, and stay motivated
💡 Big Picture: While philosophy and sociology tell us what to teach and why we educate, educational psychology provides the scientific understanding of how learning takes place — allowing educators to design age-appropriate, efficient, and effective learning environments.

🏫 Major Theories of Learning
🔔
Behaviorism
Learning = observable change in behavior
Core view
Learning is a change in observable behavior caused by external stimuli, conditioning, and reinforcement.
Key thinkers
Watson Skinner Pavlov
Key mechanisms
Classical Conditioning (Pavlov): Learning through association.
Operant Conditioning (Skinner): Learning through rewards and consequences.
Classroom application
Routine formation, direct instruction, reward systems, practice drills, and immediate feedback.
💻
Cognitivism
Learning = internal mental processing
Core view
Learning is an internal mental process involving how information is received, organized, stored, and retrieved by the brain.
Key thinkers
Piaget Bruner Gagné
Key mechanisms
Information Processing Model: Sensory memory → Working memory → Long-term memory.
Schemas: Mental structures used to organize knowledge.
Classroom application
Mind mapping, chunking information, advance organizers, graphic organizers, and scaffolded teaching.
🏗️
Constructivism
Learning = actively building understanding
Core view
Learners actively build their own understanding by linking new experiences to prior knowledge.
Key thinkers
Vygotsky Piaget von Glasersfeld
Key concepts
Cognitive Constructivism (Piaget): Stages of cognitive development.
Social Constructivism (Vygotsky): ZPD and Scaffolding.
Classroom application
Inquiry-based learning, problem-solving, group discussions, hands-on experimentation, and peer tutoring.
💖
Humanism
Learning = personal growth & self-actualization
Core view
Learning is a personal, holistic process aimed at self-actualization, personal growth, and emotional well-being.
Key thinkers
Rogers Maslow
Key concept
Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs: Basic physiological and safety needs must be met before higher-level cognitive learning can occur.
Classroom application
Student-centered learning choices, supportive classroom culture, focus on self-esteem, self-evaluation, and emotional safety.
📈 Human Growth and Development Stages
Early Childhood
2–7 years
Piaget: Preoperational — symbolic thought, egocentrism, language development.
💡 Educational needs: Play-based learning, multi-sensory materials, basic social interactions.
Middle Childhood
7–11 years
Piaget: Concrete Operational — logical thinking about concrete objects, conservation.
💡 Educational needs: Hands-on experiments, structured group tasks, clear logical rules.
Adolescence
12+ years
Piaget: Formal Operational — abstract reasoning, hypothetical thinking, metacognition.
💡 Educational needs: Critical analysis, debate, self-directed research, career planning.
🔑 Key Factors Influencing Learning
🔥 Motivation
Intrinsic Motivation: Learning driven by internal curiosity, passion, and personal satisfaction.

Extrinsic Motivation: Learning driven by external rewards, grades, recognition, or avoiding negative consequences.

🎨 Individual Differences & Learning Styles
Multiple Intelligences (Gardner): Students possess different types of intelligence — Linguistic, Logical-Mathematical, Spatial, Musical, Bodily-Kinesthetic, Interpersonal, Intrapersonal, Naturalistic.

Inclusive Education: Differentiating instruction to support diverse abilities, learning differences, and special educational needs (SEN).

🔑 Key Takeaway: Educational psychology gives teachers the scientific tools to match instruction to how students actually learn. No single theory explains everything — effective teachers draw from behaviorism for routines, cognitivism for memory, constructivism for deep understanding, and humanism for motivation and well-being.
