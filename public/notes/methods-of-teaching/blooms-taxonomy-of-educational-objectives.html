🎯 Methods of Teaching

Bloom's Taxonomy of Educational Objectives

A ladder of learning — from simple recall to creative thinking
💡 What is it? Bloom's Taxonomy is a classification system that arranges learning objectives from simple to complex. It helps teachers decide what students should learn and how deeply they should learn it. Developed by Benjamin Bloom and colleagues (1956) and revised by Anderson & Krathwohl (2001).

🌟 Why It Matters & Key Facts
📝
Why It's Important
Helps teachers write clear objectives · Moves teaching from memorisation to thinking skills · Helps plan lessons, activities, and tests · Makes assessment match what was taught · Shows students how to grow from knowledge to creativity.
📅
Origins & Revision
Developed by: Benjamin Bloom & team, University of Chicago. Published: 1956, Taxonomy of Educational Objectives. Revised: 2001, by Lorin Anderson & David Krathwohl.
🧠
Three Domains of Learning
Cognitive – mental skills ("head") Affective – feelings & attitudes ("heart") Psychomotor – physical skills ("hands") The cognitive domain is the most widely used.

⚖️ Lower-Order vs Higher-Order Thinking

📘 LOTS — Lower-Order Thinking Skills

  • Remember
  • Understand
  • Apply (sometimes counted here)
Good teaching begins with LOTS but should not stop there.

🚀 HOTS — Higher-Order Thinking Skills

  • Analyse
  • Evaluate
  • Create
Teaching should move students toward HOTS for deeper learning.
📚 Revised Taxonomy: The Knowledge Dimension
📌 Factual
Basic facts and terms — the building blocks of knowledge.
Examples: names, dates, vocabulary
💡 Conceptual
Ideas, categories, and theories — how facts relate to each other.
Example: what democracy means
🔧 Procedural
How to do something — methods, steps, and techniques.
Example: steps of a science experiment
🧭 Metacognitive
Awareness of your own thinking and learning — knowing how you learn best.
Example: knowing your best study method
❤️ The Affective Domain (Krathwohl, 1964)
1
Receiving
Being willing to listen or pay attention.
2
Responding
Actively taking part.
3
Valuing
Seeing worth in something.
4
Organising
Building a personal value system.
5
Characterising
Acting consistently on those values.
📖 Example: A student listens to a talk on honesty (receiving), discusses it (responding), believes it matters (valuing), compares it with other values (organising), and always acts honestly (characterising).
🤲 The Psychomotor Domain

This domain deals with physical and practical skills. Bloom's team did not complete it. Later, Simpson (1972) and Dave (1970) developed it.

Perception Set Guided Response Mechanism Complex Overt Response Adaptation Origination
📖 Example — Learning to write: watching a model → copying strokes with help → practising until smooth → developing a personal handwriting style.
🧑‍🏫 How Teachers Use Bloom's Taxonomy
✍️ Writing Objectives
Use measurable verbs to make objectives clear and assessable.
Weak: "Students will know about photosynthesis."
Better: "Students will explain the process of photosynthesis."
📅 Planning Lessons
Include activities at different levels — don't stay only at Remember and Understand.
📊 Designing Assessment
Mix easy, medium, and challenging questions so all levels are tested.
❓ Questioning in Class
Ask questions from all levels, not only "what" questions.
🎯 Differentiation
Give struggling learners support at lower levels and challenge advanced learners with higher levels.

⚖️ Advantages & Criticisms

✅ Advantages

  • Simple, clear, and widely accepted
  • Encourages deep learning and critical thinking
  • Helps align objectives, teaching, and assessment
  • Useful for all subjects and grade levels

⚠️ Criticisms & Limitations

  • Learning is not always strictly step-by-step — students may create before fully mastering earlier levels
  • Levels can overlap, so it is sometimes hard to place a task in one level
  • Focuses mainly on thinking and may ignore culture, context, and emotions
  • Verbs like "describe" or "explain" can fit more than one level depending on the task
"Really Useful Aids Always Enhance Creativity"
Remember → Understand → Apply → Analyse → Evaluate → Create
