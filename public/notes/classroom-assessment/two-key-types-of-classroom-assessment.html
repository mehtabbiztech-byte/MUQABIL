Two Key Types of Classroom Assessment

Formative assessment steers the journey; summative assessment reports on the destination

⏱️ 10 min read 📚 7 sections 🔑 12 key terms 📝 10 MCQs · 4 CRQs · 1 ERQ

The big idea

Classroom assessment is conventionally divided into two key types by purpose: formative assessment, which happens during learning and is used to adjust teaching and give feedback, and summative assessment, which happens at the end of a period of learning and is used to judge and report achievement. Formative is assessment *for* learning; summative is assessment *of* learning. The distinction is about purpose and use, not about the instrument: the same quiz can be formative if its results change tomorrow’s lesson, and summative if it is graded for the report. A third type, diagnostic assessment, is usually treated as a specialised form of formative assessment carried out before teaching, and is examined alongside the two key types.

What you will master

  1. Define formative and summative assessment by purpose, timing and use, and give two classroom examples of each.
  2. Explain why the same instrument can be formative or summative, and what determines which it becomes.
  3. Show how formative evidence should feed summative judgements, and how diagnostic assessment fits the two-type model.

Foundations

Core concepts

Each card isolates one idea examiners test. Read the definition first, then the In practice line — that is where scenario questions begin.

🧭 01

Formative assessment: assessment for learning

Formative assessment is any evidence-gathering during learning that is interpreted and used to adapt teaching and give feedback. Its defining feature is consequence: the learner or teacher does something differently because of it. Typical tools are hinge questions, exit tickets, mini-whiteboards, cold calling, drafts with comments, peer and self-assessment, and targeted observation.

In practice

Plan one hinge question per lesson with a distractor for each common misconception, then act on the result.

🏁 02

Summative assessment: assessment of learning

Summative assessment summarises achievement at the end of a unit, term, year or stage for reporting, certification or progression. It demands coverage, comparability and defensible marking, so tests, projects with rubrics and moderated tasks are used. Its results are usually recorded and communicated to learners, parents or authorities.

In practice

Build summative papers from a table of specifications so marks match the taught outcomes and their weight.

🔁 03

Purpose, not instrument, decides the type

The same 10-question quiz is formative when it is ungraded and used to regroup learners, and summative when it counts towards a term grade. This is why labelling a task ‘formative’ in a lesson plan is meaningless unless the plan says how the evidence will be used.

In practice

For every assessment, write the use sentence: ‘If learners get X wrong, I will…’

🔍 04

Diagnostic assessment: finding the cause

Diagnostic assessment occurs before teaching to establish prior knowledge and locate specific misconceptions, so that instruction starts at the right point. It is close to formative in purpose because it changes teaching, but it looks backwards at learning that should already exist. Error analysis and short ungraded probes are its main tools.

In practice

Analyse wrong answers by error type rather than counting them; the pattern tells you what to teach.

📈 05

How formative evidence feeds summative judgement

Formative and summative are strongest when connected. Formative evidence shows which outcomes are secure, which learners need support, and where a summative task would be unfair or badly designed. Summative judgement should therefore be built from a documented record of formative evidence, not from one terminal paper.

In practice

Keep a one-page outcome tracker; tick formative evidence against outcomes, then use it to build the term judgement.

⚖️ 06

Balancing the two in a real classroom

Over-emphasis on summative assessment narrows teaching, raises anxiety and encourages mark-chasing. Over-emphasis on formative assessment without periodic summative judgement leaves parents and learners without an honest account of achievement. A defensible classroom has frequent low-stakes formative checks and periodic, well-designed summative assessments, with feedback in both directions.

In practice

Aim for a 4:1 ratio of formative to summative activity in a unit, and tell learners which is which.

Precision

Key vocabulary

Be able to define each term in one sentence. Bold terms below also appear highlighted inside the notes, so you can meet them in context.

Formative assessment
Assessment during learning, used to adapt teaching and give feedback; assessment for learning.
Summative assessment
Assessment at the end of a period of learning, used to summarise and report achievement.
Diagnostic assessment
Pre-teaching assessment that identifies prior knowledge and specific misconceptions.
Assessment for learning
The use of assessment evidence by learners and teachers to decide where to go next.
Assessment of learning
The use of assessment to summarise and certify what has been learned.
Hinge question
A single diagnostic question used mid-lesson to decide whether to move on or reteach.
Exit ticket
A short end-of-lesson task that captures each learner’s understanding for the next lesson.
Feedback
Information about performance against criteria that directs the next step.
Table of specifications
A blueprint mapping marks to content areas and cognitive levels when building a test.
Moderation
Agreement of judgements across teachers to ensure comparable standards.
Reliability
Consistency of an assessment’s results across occasions, forms and markers.
Validity
The extent to which an assessment measures the intended learning outcomes.

04

Practice

Classroom application

What to do, and what to avoid, in a real classroom. The left column is the standard to teach; the right column lists the errors that cost marks and learning.

✅ Do this

  • Name each assessment as formative or summative in your plan, with its intended use.
  • Use hinge questions and exit tickets to make formative evidence routine and fast.
  • Give feedback against success criteria so learners know the next step.
  • Design summative papers from a table of specifications that mirrors taught outcomes.
  • Base summative judgements on a documented record of formative evidence.
  • Tell learners which tasks are practice and which count, so they can take sensible risks.

🚫 Avoid this

  • Do not label a task formative if its results never change teaching.
  • Do not grade every piece of formative work; it turns practice into evaluation.
  • Do not let summative assessment be the only evidence you can produce at reporting time.
  • Do not design summative papers by choosing items that are easy to mark.
  • Do not use diagnostic assessment to rank learners; use it to locate misconceptions.
  • Do not keep formative evidence only in your head — record it against outcomes in one place.
05

Exam traps

Common misconceptions

Each misconception below is a plausible-sounding statement that loses marks. Learn the reality column as a correction you can write.

Myth

“Formative assessment means ungraded work, and summative means graded work.”

✔ Reality

The distinction is purpose and use, not grading. A graded quiz whose results are analysed and used for reteaching is functioning formatively; an ungraded test whose results are filed away is not.

Myth

“Formative assessment is optional extra work once the syllabus is covered.”

✔ Reality

Formative assessment is the mechanism by which coverage becomes learning. Without it, the teacher has no evidence about whether the syllabus was understood until the summative results arrive — too late to act.

Myth

“Summative assessment is the enemy of good teaching.”

✔ Reality

Well-designed summative assessment is necessary for honest reporting, progression and public accountability. The problem is over-use and poor design, not the purpose itself.

Myth

“Diagnostic assessment is a separate third type unrelated to the other two.”

✔ Reality

It is usually treated as a specialised form of formative assessment used before teaching; its purpose — adapting instruction — is formative, while its focus is prior learning.

06

Compress

Key takeaways

Revise these lines the night before the paper — they compress the whole note into recall-ready statements.

Six things to remember

  1. Formative assessment happens during learning and changes the next step; summative assessment summarises at the end.
  2. The type is decided by purpose and use, not by the instrument or the presence of marks.
  3. Diagnostic assessment locates prior knowledge and is formative in purpose.
  4. Formative evidence should feed the summative judgement so that reporting is defensible.
  5. Summative papers need a table of specifications for valid coverage and weighting.
  6. Frequent low-stakes formative checks plus periodic, well-designed summative tasks is the defensible balance.
One-line summary

Formative assessment is assessment for learning — continuous, low-stakes and used to adjust teaching and give feedback — while summative assessment is assessment of learning, used at the end of a period to judge and report achievement against criteria.

💡
Exam tip

If a question says ‘the teacher analyses the quiz and regroups learners’, the answer is formative even though a quiz looks like a test. If it says ‘used for the report card’ or ‘promotion’, the answer is summative.
