Assessment Policies and Practices in Pakistani Schools

From classroom exit tickets to board examinations — who assesses what, why, and with what consequence

⏱️ 11 min read 📚 7 sections 🔑 12 key terms 📝 10 MCQs · 4 CRQs · 1 ERQ

The big idea

Assessment in Pakistan operates at several levels at once. The federal government sets the curriculum framework and national priorities, provincial governments run school systems and textbook boards, examination boards certify achievement at Grades 9–12, provincial examination commissions have assessed Grade 5 and Grade 8, and national bodies sample learning for system monitoring. Inside the classroom, the teacher’s own tests and marking remain the dominant day-to-day instrument. Policy since the Single National Curriculum (SNC) has emphasised standards, benchmarks and student learning outcomes, and has encouraged continuous and formative assessment — but high-stakes public examinations still shape what is taught. Understanding this architecture, and the gap between policy and practice, is what examination questions on ‘assessment policies and practices’ actually test.

What you will master

  1. Map the levels of assessment in Pakistan: classroom, school, provincial, board and national, with the purpose and consequence of each.
  2. Explain what the Single National Curriculum changed in assessment expectations and why implementation has been uneven.
  3. Evaluate the effects of high-stakes public examinations on classroom practice, and propose realistic improvements.

Foundations

Core concepts

Each card isolates one idea examiners test. Read the definition first, then the In practice line — that is where scenario questions begin.

🏛️ 01

The policy architecture: who decides what

Curriculum and national priorities are set at federal level through the Ministry of Federal Education and Professional Training, with provincial governments responsible for school delivery, teacher employment and textbook boards. Examination boards (BISE provincially, FBISE federally) certify Grades 9–12, while provincial commissions and national bodies conduct large-scale assessment. Classroom assessment is delegated entirely to teachers, which is why practice varies so widely between schools.

In practice

When a policy question appears, name the level first: classroom, school, district, province, board or nation.

📘 02

SNC and the shift to outcomes and continuous assessment

The Single National Curriculum, introduced from 2020 and phased across grades, is organised around competencies, standards, benchmarks and student learning outcomes (SLOs), and it explicitly encourages formative and continuous assessment rather than reliance on a single terminal paper. The SLO format is significant: it makes the criterion for assessment visible and obliges teachers to assess what the outcome states, not merely what the textbook contains.

In practice

Take one SLO from the curriculum and write a two-item check that would show whether it has been achieved.

🏆 03

Public examinations and their washback

Board examinations at Grades 9–12 are high-stakes: they determine progression, college admission and employment prospects. Their washback is strong — teaching narrows to tested content, past papers dominate, and subjects or skills not examined are marginalised. Marking is centralised and often rushed, and reliability depends on marking schemes, examiner training and moderation.

In practice

Teach one examined topic through an unseen-source question rather than past-paper recall, and discuss the difference with learners.

📊 04

Large-scale and sample-based assessment

The National Education Assessment System (NEAS) and participation in international studies such as TIMSS sample learning nationally to inform policy, not to grade individuals. Provincial bodies have also run Grade 5 and Grade 8 examinations — in Punjab through the Punjab Examination Commission (PEC) — although arrangements have changed over time. System-level assessment answers ‘how is the system doing?’, a different question from ‘how is this learner doing?’.

In practice

Distinguish clearly in your answers between assessment for certification, for improvement and for system monitoring.

🏫 05

Classroom practice: what actually happens

In most classrooms, assessment means teacher-made monthly and terminal tests, exercise-book marking and annual examinations, often in large classes with heavy marking loads. Feedback is frequently reduced to a tick, a date or a mark. Promotion practices vary: some provinces have restricted detention in early grades, which removes a consequence of failure without replacing it with formative support.

In practice

Audit your own assessment week: how much of it produced a change in teaching, and how much produced only a number?

🔧 06

Challenges and reform directions

The recurring challenges are assessment literacy in pre-service training, marking load, weak moderation, examination security, tuition culture and the dominance of recall items. Reform directions include stronger formative assessment in teacher education, item banking and moderation, use of assessment data for school improvement, and building classroom assessment that is credible enough to reduce the pressure on a single terminal paper.

In practice

Propose one change a school could make next term that improves assessment without new budget.

Precision

Key vocabulary

Be able to define each term in one sentence. Bold terms below also appear highlighted inside the notes, so you can meet them in context.

Single National Curriculum (SNC)
National curriculum framework introduced from 2020, organised around competencies, standards, benchmarks and student learning outcomes.
Student learning outcome (SLO)
A specific, observable statement of what a learner should know or be able to do by the end of a stage.
BISE
Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education; conducts SSC and HSSC examinations in each province.
FBISE
Federal Board of Intermediate and Secondary Education; examines federal institutions and affiliated schools.
PEC
Punjab Examination Commission, which has conducted Grade 5 and Grade 8 examinations in Punjab.
NEAS
National Education Assessment System; sample-based national assessment used for system monitoring.
TIMSS
Trends in International Mathematics and Science Study; an international sample-based assessment in which Pakistan has participated.
Washback
The effect of an examination on teaching and learning, usually narrowing content to what is tested.
Moderation
Process of comparing and agreeing marks across examiners or schools to ensure consistent standards.
Marking scheme
The documented allocation of marks and acceptable answers used to score a paper consistently.
No-detention policy
A policy that prevents learners being held back in certain early grades, used in some provincial systems.
Assessment literacy
The knowledge and skill teachers need to design, interpret and use assessment evidence well.

04

Practice

Classroom application

What to do, and what to avoid, in a real classroom. The left column is the standard to teach; the right column lists the errors that cost marks and learning.

✅ Do this

  • Identify the level of assessment before discussing its purpose or consequence.
  • Plan classroom assessment against the curriculum’s student learning outcomes.
  • Use formative assessment to reduce dependence on the single terminal paper.
  • Prepare learners for public examinations while still teaching untested skills such as inquiry and communication.
  • Share marking schemes or rubrics with learners so criteria are transparent.
  • Use school-level data to target support rather than only to rank classes.

🚫 Avoid this

  • Do not describe all assessment in Pakistan as if it were board examination.
  • Do not present sample-based national assessment as if it graded individual learners.
  • Do not assume a curriculum document changes classroom practice without teacher support.
  • Do not teach only past-paper items; washback narrows learning and often lowers performance on unfamiliar tasks.
  • Do not treat a promotion policy as an assessment policy; removing detention is not the same as providing support.
  • Do not cite a specific examination arrangement as permanent — provincial policies change and should be checked.
05

Exam traps

Common misconceptions

Each misconception below is a plausible-sounding statement that loses marks. Learn the reality column as a correction you can write.

Myth

“Assessment in Pakistan means the board examination at the end of Grade 10.”

✔ Reality

Board examinations are one level. Classroom formative assessment, school term examinations, provincial Grade 5 and 8 assessments and national sample-based studies all form part of the system, with different purposes and consequences.

Myth

“The Single National Curriculum replaced examinations with continuous assessment.”

✔ Reality

The SNC changed the curriculum’s structure and its assessment expectations, emphasising outcomes and formative assessment, but public examinations continue and still exert strong washback on teaching.

Myth

“National assessment results are used to fail individual learners.”

✔ Reality

NEAS and international studies such as TIMSS are sample-based and report at system level; their purpose is monitoring and policy, not individual certification.

Myth

“If the curriculum states an outcome, teachers will automatically assess it.”

✔ Reality

Implementation depends on assessment literacy, resources, class size and the pressure of public examinations. Policy intention and classroom practice are frequently far apart.

06

Compress

Key takeaways

Revise these lines the night before the paper — they compress the whole note into recall-ready statements.

Six things to remember

  1. Assessment in Pakistan operates at classroom, school, provincial, board and national levels with different purposes.
  2. The SNC organised the curriculum around competencies, standards, benchmarks and SLOs and encouraged formative assessment.
  3. Board examinations remain high-stakes and produce strong washback on what is taught.
  4. Provincial commissions and NEAS assess samples or cohorts for accountability and system monitoring.
  5. Classroom practice is dominated by teacher-made tests and exercise-book marking, often with little feedback.
  6. The central reform need is assessment literacy and credible classroom assessment, not more testing.
One-line summary

Assessment policy in Pakistan spans national curriculum outcomes, provincial and board examinations and sample-based national monitoring, while classroom practice remains dominated by teacher-made tests whose feedback potential is largely unrealised.

💡
Exam tip

Policy questions reward precise levels and purposes. If the stem mentions certification or college admission, it is board examination; if it mentions system performance or sampling, it is NEAS or TIMSS; if it mentions SLOs, it is the SNC framework.
