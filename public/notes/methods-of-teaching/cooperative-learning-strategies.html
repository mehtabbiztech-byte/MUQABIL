🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Methods of Teaching

Cooperative Learning

Breaking students into small groups to learn together
💡 Core Idea: Cooperative learning centers around breaking students into small groups to learn. This allows both individual students and the class at large to grasp concepts through their personal learning styles and those of fellow students. What separates it from other strategies is that each group member has a separate task to complete or concept to explain — so individual learning benefits the whole group.
🔍
8 strategies
🔑 What Makes It Cooperative?
👥
Small Group Learning
Students are broken into small groups. Both individual students and the class grasp concepts through their personal learning styles and those of fellow students.
🎯
Individual Accountability
Each group member has a separate task or concept to explain. Individual learning benefits the whole group, so students work toward mastery together.
🧑‍🏫
Teacher as Facilitator
Students lead the learning process. The teacher acts more as a facilitator, helping students understand how to connect the pieces.
⚙️ 8 Cooperative Learning Strategies
1
Speed Quizzes
Give each student a flashcard containing a question and an answer. Break students into groups for a round of virtual speed quizzing, moving from one student's question to the next in quick succession.
📌 Example
In a biology revision session, each student gets a flashcard (e.g., "What is mitosis?"). Groups race through all questions, with each member reading their question aloud and peers answering before the next card.
2
Puzzle Pieces
Assign each group member one aspect of a larger project. In doing so, group members will have to communicate with one another to put the pieces together.
📌 Example
For a group presentation on climate change, one student researches causes, another covers effects, a third explores solutions, and a fourth designs the slides. They must meet to assemble a complete picture.
3
One-Minute Papers
To assess how well a student comprehends recently-discussed classroom material, have each student write a quick one-minute paper at the beginning of class before diving back into the topic.
📌 Example
At the start of a history lesson, students spend one minute writing what they remember about the previous day's topic (e.g., causes of WWI). Then they compare notes with a partner before the new lesson begins.
4
Sentence Completion
As a writing exercise, have students work together to complete sentences on a given topic. One student begins the sentence and then passes it along to the next student in the group. Once complete, the team can review it together for clarity and comprehension.
📌 Example
In an English class, one student writes "The main theme of the novel is..." and passes it on. The next adds "...which is shown when..." and so on, until the group has a full paragraph they review together.
5
Classmate Interviews
Divide students into groups of three. Task one student with writing questions and interviewing another student, while the third student is responsible for taking notes on the interview.
📌 Example
In a civics class, Student A interviews Student B about their views on voting age, while Student C takes notes. Then they rotate roles so everyone experiences each part of the process.
6
Student Expertise
Have students lean on one another to expand their respective levels of expertise. This is particularly effective when one student or group of students grasps certain concepts sooner than others.
📌 Example
In a math class, a student who quickly understands quadratic equations becomes the "class expert" and helps peers who are still struggling, reinforcing their own understanding in the process.
7
Freeride Elimination
Make sure every student is accountable for a specific aspect of a project by "freeride-proofing" the collaboration process. Give each group member a colored marker and a to-do list. By the end of the group work session, there should be a checkmark next to each task, with all the colors represented.
📌 Example
During a group poster project, each student uses a different colored marker to sign off on the tasks they completed. The teacher can instantly see who contributed what — and no one can hide.
8
Teamwork 101
Before sending your students off to work in groups, make sure they understand what teamwork is and how to work together.
📌 Example
At the start of the year, the teacher runs a mini-lesson on active listening, sharing ideas fairly, and resolving disagreements. Students practice these skills in a low-stakes group activity before real projects begin.
🌟 Benefits of Cooperative Learning
📚 Improves how well students comprehend information
😊 Improves how students feel about school, teachers, and classmates
💪 Boosts self-esteem and confidence
🧠 Builds social skills, patience, time management, leadership, and critical thinking
🎓 Creates student-centered learning opportunities
👀 Helps teachers see and understand how their students think
🔄 Helps students understand that others may think differently
🛠️ Provides hands-on and applied learning opportunities
🌈 Creates a more inclusive classroom community
🌍 Teaches in an environment students will encounter throughout life
✋ Reduces disengagement and minimizes classroom management issues
☕ Gives students a break from listening and a chance to refocus
⚡ Ease of implementation
🏆 Improves a student's level of achievement
✨ Provides more authentic learning experiences
🔥 Increases motivation and personal interdependence
🎯 Simultaneously engages as many students as possible
🔑 Key Takeaway: Cooperative learning works best when every student has a meaningful role. It's not just group work — it's structured interdependence where individual success contributes to group success.
