Teacher-centred & Student-centred Approaches

Two ends of a spectrum — and how good classrooms blend them
💡 Big Picture: Teacher-centred and student-centred approaches sit at opposite ends of a spectrum, and most good classrooms blend them. In practice, effective teachers move between the two depending on the objective, the learners, and the context.

🎯 The Two Approaches
👨‍🏫
Teacher-centred
Expert & transmitter
📖 What it is
The teacher is the main source of knowledge and controls the pace, content, and activities. Students are mostly receivers of information.
🧰 Common methods
Lecture Direct instruction Demonstration Drill & practice Recitation Textbook-based
✅ Strengths
  • Efficient for delivering large amounts of content in limited time
  • Works well with large classes and limited resources
  • Gives clear structure and predictability
  • Suits foundational skills (phonics, arithmetic facts, safety procedures)
⚠️ Limitations
  • Students can become passive and attention often drops
  • Encourages memorisation more than deep understanding
  • Little room for individual differences or student voice
  • Weak at developing critical thinking and collaboration
🧑‍🎓
Student-centred
Facilitator & guide
📖 What it is
The teacher acts as a facilitator or guide, and students take an active role in constructing knowledge through inquiry, discussion, and doing.
🧰 Common methods
Inquiry-based learning Project-based learning Problem-based learning Cooperative learning Discussions & debates Learning centres Peer teaching Differentiated instruction
✅ Strengths
  • Builds critical thinking, problem-solving, and communication skills
  • Increases motivation and ownership of learning
  • Accommodates different learning styles, paces, and interests
  • Supports deeper, longer-lasting understanding
⚠️ Limitations
  • Needs more planning, time, and materials
  • Harder to manage in very large classes
  • Can leave gaps in coverage if not well structured
  • Depends on students having enough prior knowledge and learning habits
📊 Key Differences at a Glance
Feature 👨‍🏫 Teacher-centred 🧑‍🎓 Student-centred
Teacher's role Expert, transmitter Facilitator, guide
Student's role Passive receiver Active participant
Focus Content and curriculum Learner needs and process
Interaction Mostly teacher to student Teacher-student & student-student
Assessment Tests, recall of facts Projects, portfolios, performance, reflection
Classroom feel Quiet, orderly Busy, collaborative
📚 Theoretical Roots
🧠 Teacher-centred: Behaviourism
Learning is shaped through reinforcement and repetition. The teacher provides stimuli and reinforcement to shape student behaviour.
Skinner Pavlov
🌱 Student-centred: Constructivism & Humanism
Learners build meaning through experience, social interaction, and support within their zone of proximal development.
Piaget Vygotsky Bruner Rogers Dewey
⚖️ A Balanced View
🎯 In practice, effective teachers move between the two depending on the objective, the learners, and the context. A short direct explanation can set up a group task; a student-led project can end with a teacher-led consolidation. In many settings, including large classes and resource-limited schools, a gradual shift (such as adding think-pair-share or small-group tasks to an otherwise lecture-based lesson) is more realistic than a complete overhaul.
